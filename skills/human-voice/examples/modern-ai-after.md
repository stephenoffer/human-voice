# Making one-on-ones worth the half hour

Two rules cover most of what goes wrong. Never let it become a status meeting, and never cancel it.

The status version is the easier failure to notice, because you can feel it while it is happening. You are asking somebody to say out loud what they already wrote down, which converts a document into a slower document, and neither of you agreed to protect the slot for that. The conversations that justify the half hour are the ones that would never have appeared in a written update at all.

Cancelling is the harder failure, since it always looks reasonable at the time. The weeks you cancel are the busy ones, and the busy ones are usually the weeks worth talking about. A recurring thirty minutes that never moves builds an expectation that there will be a place to raise something, and that expectation is most of what eventually makes people raise it.

Let the agenda belong to them. Arrive with your own list every week and you have quietly built a second channel for handing out work, at which point the other person stops bringing anything of their own. Ask for their items first and keep yours until the end.

Some weeks there is genuinely nothing, which is fine. Letting a quiet minute sit is better than filling it with a question neither of you cares about, and over a year the meeting earns its place through the handful of times it was already there when something did come up.
