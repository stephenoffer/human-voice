# Ship your changelog without writing it

Your release notes are already written. They are sitting in your merge commits, your issue titles, and the Slack thread where someone explained what actually changed.

Notchup reads your repository and drafts the changelog entry when you tag a release. You review it, edit the parts that matter, and publish. What used to be a Friday afternoon of archaeology becomes a five-minute review.

Teams tell us the real value is not the time saved. It is that the changelog finally gets published at all. When writing it is a chore that falls to whoever has the least on their plate, it slips, and then it slips again, and eventually customers stop looking.

Notchup connects to GitHub and GitLab, supports self-hosted instances, and publishes to a hosted page or your own site. Start with the free tier for public repositories, and move to a paid plan when you need private ones.
