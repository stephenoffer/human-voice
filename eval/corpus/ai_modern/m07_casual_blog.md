# What I learned running a book club for two years

The hardest part of running a book club is not choosing books. It is accepting that most people will not finish them, and building something that still works when they haven't.

We started with the obvious approach: pick a book, meet in a month, discuss. Attendance dropped from twelve to four by the third month. The people who came had finished the book and the people who hadn't stayed home, embarrassed. That is the failure mode nobody warns you about, because the problem looks like a lack of commitment when it is really a design flaw.

What fixed it was splitting the discussion. The first half covers the opening third of the book, which almost everyone reads, and the second half goes deeper for whoever kept going. Attendance recovered and stayed. People who read forty pages still have something to say, and saying it makes them more likely to finish next time.

The other change that mattered was picking shorter books. Two hundred pages is a commitment people keep. Four hundred is an aspiration.
