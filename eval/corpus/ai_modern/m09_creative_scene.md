The house had been empty for three years, and it showed in the way the air held still when Marta opened the door. Dust had settled into a fine even layer on the banister, undisturbed, the way snow lies on a road nobody drives.

She had expected to feel something. That was the reason she had driven four hours and let herself in with a key she was not sure still worked. But standing in the hallway, what she felt was mostly the practical weight of the thing: the smell of damp somewhere upstairs, the yellowed envelopes fanned across the floor beneath the mail slot, the certainty that the boiler would need replacing before anyone could live here again.

Her mother's coat was still on the hook by the stairs. Marta looked at it for a while and then did not touch it. There would be time for that, or there would not be, and either way it was not going to happen in the first ten minutes.

She went to the kitchen and turned the tap. The water ran brown, then clear.
