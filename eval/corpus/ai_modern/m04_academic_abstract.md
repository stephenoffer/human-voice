# Effects of intermittent feedback on task persistence

This study examines whether the timing of performance feedback influences how long participants persist on an unsolvable puzzle task. Prior work has established that feedback frequency affects motivation, but the direction of the effect remains contested, in part because studies differ in how they operationalize persistence.

We randomly assigned 214 undergraduate participants to receive feedback after every trial, after every fourth trial, or not at all. Persistence was measured as time on task before voluntary withdrawal. Participants in the intermittent condition persisted substantially longer than those in either the continuous or no-feedback conditions, and the difference remained after controlling for baseline task interest.

Two interpretations are consistent with the data. Intermittent feedback may sustain uncertainty about eventual success, which maintains effort. Alternatively, the reduced information rate may simply slow the accumulation of evidence that the task is unsolvable. The present design cannot distinguish these accounts, and doing so would require manipulating feedback informativeness independently of its frequency.

The findings have practical implications for instructional design, though we would caution against generalizing from an artificial task to sustained academic work.
