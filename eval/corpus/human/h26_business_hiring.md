# Notes on our last twelve engineering hires

Twelve hires, fourteen months. Here is what the data says and where I disagree
with it.

Nine came from referrals. Two came from a job board. One came from a
conference talk that our CTO gave in Lisbon. The referral hires ramped faster
by about three weeks on average, measured by first meaningful PR merged, which
is a crude metric but the only one we tracked consistently.

The obvious read is: double down on referrals. I think that read is wrong, or
at least incomplete. Referrals ramp faster partly because they arrive knowing
someone, and that person answers their questions in DMs. It is not that they
are better engineers. It is that we have an informal onboarding system that
only works if you already have a friend here.

Our two job board hires took the longest to ramp and are now, eighteen months
in, two of the four people I would least like to lose. Neither of them knew
anybody. Both of them wrote down what they had to figure out, and those
documents are now what we hand new people.

Attrition: we lost two of the twelve. One left after five months for a
significantly larger offer, which I do not think we could have matched or
should have. The other left after nine and told me in the exit conversation
that the work was not what the job description described. That one is on me. I
wrote the description.

If I were doing it again I would keep the referral pipeline and stop treating
its ramp advantage as a signal about candidate quality. The advantage is about
us, not them.
