# What I learned from tracking my reading for a year

I logged every book I read last year, mostly to find out whether my sense of my
own habits matched the record. It did not. The gap was more interesting than
the total.

I assumed I would read fewer books than I thought. I read 34, which is more
than I would have guessed. My estimate was low because I had only been counting
the ones I talked to somebody about. Books I read and never mentioned had
quietly fallen out of my own accounting of the year.

The distribution is where it got uncomfortable. Nineteen of the 34 came in
three two-week clusters; the other fifteen were spread thin across ten months.
Those clusters map onto travel and onto a stretch in February when a project
stalled and I had long empty evenings. So my reading runs on available blocks
of time, not on intention. That is a less flattering story than the one I had
been telling myself.

I also tracked abandonment, and that is the column I would tell anyone else to
keep. I started 51 and finished 34. Looking down the list of seventeen I
dropped, nearly all of them were books I picked up because I felt I should.
The ones I finished were the ones I wanted.

Will I keep doing this? Probably not at this detail. Everything useful showed
up in the first six months and the rest was spreadsheet maintenance. But I am
keeping the abandonment column.
