Three years empty, and the house told on itself the moment Marta opened the door.
The air didn't move. Dust lay along the banister in one fine even sheet, the way
snow lies on a road nobody drives.

She had expected to feel something. That was why she'd driven four hours and let
herself in with a key she wasn't sure would still turn. What she felt in the hallway
was mostly logistics: damp somewhere upstairs, yellowed envelopes fanned across the
floor under the mail slot, the boiler that would have to be replaced before anyone
could live here.

Her mother's coat hung on the hook by the stairs. She looked at it. She didn't touch
it. There would be time for that or there wouldn't, and either way it wasn't going
to happen in the first ten minutes.

In the kitchen she turned the tap. Brown, then clear.
