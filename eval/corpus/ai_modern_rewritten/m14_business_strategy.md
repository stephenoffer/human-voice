# Expanding into the mid-market segment

Enterprise accounts are 78% of our revenue. Organizations above 5,000 employees
pay us well, and losing one of them costs about 4% of ARR overnight. There are
roughly 2,400 addressable enterprises in our category. We already serve 190, so
the ceiling is visible from here.

Mid-market means 200 to 5,000 employees, and there are about 31,000 of them.

Size is not the argument, though. The sales cycle is. We closed eleven
mid-market accounts opportunistically last year and they averaged 34 days from
first contact to signature. Enterprise averaged 187. That difference is the
whole case, and it is the part of the case I trust, because we did not design
the pilot to prove anything and it came out that way anyway.

So why haven't we done this? Our product assumes an enterprise deployment: a
dedicated administrator, SSO configuration, and a data migration that eats
about two weeks of professional services time. Mid-market buyers do not have a
dedicated administrator and will not pay for services. Our onboarding path
disqualifies the segment before a salesperson ever gets involved.

Three things have to change. Self-service onboarding replaces guided setup, so
a customer reaches first value without a call. Billing moves from annual to
monthly. Support moves from named account managers to a pool, because the
current cost structure does not survive a lower average contract value.

Call it $2.1 million over two quarters, almost all engineering headcount for
the self-service work. Break-even lands at 340 mid-market accounts: five to
seven quarters at the pilot conversion rate.

One risk is worth planning against. Enterprise slips while everyone's attention
is somewhere else. I do not think that risk is small.
