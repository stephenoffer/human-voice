# Effects of intermittent feedback on task persistence

Does feedback timing change how long people keep working on a problem they cannot
solve? Prior work agrees that feedback frequency affects motivation and disagrees
about the sign, largely because persistence gets operationalized differently in
every study.

We assigned 214 undergraduates to feedback after every trial, after every fourth
trial, or none, and measured time on an unsolvable puzzle before voluntary
withdrawal. The intermittent group persisted substantially longer than either other
condition. Controlling for baseline task interest left that difference intact.

Two readings fit. Intermittent feedback may sustain uncertainty about eventual
success, and uncertainty sustains effort. Or the slower information rate may simply
delay the moment a participant accumulates enough evidence to conclude the task is
rigged. We cannot separate these here. Doing so requires manipulating how
informative the feedback is, independently of how often it arrives, which this
design does not do.

The instructional-design implication is obvious and we would not lean on it. An
artificial unsolvable puzzle is not a semester of coursework.
