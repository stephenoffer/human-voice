# What I learned running a book club for two years

Choosing books is the easy part. The hard part is accepting that most people won't
finish them, and building something that still works anyway.

We did the obvious thing first. Pick a book, meet in a month, discuss it. Twelve
people became four by month three. The ones who'd finished showed up and the ones
who hadn't stayed home, embarrassed, which reads like a commitment problem and is
actually a design problem.

Splitting the discussion fixed it. First half covers the opening third, which
nearly everyone reads. Second half goes deeper for whoever kept going. Attendance
came back and stayed. Somebody who read forty pages still has something to say, and
having said it, they're likelier to finish the next one.

Shorter books helped too. Two hundred pages is a promise people keep. Four hundred
is a promise they make.
